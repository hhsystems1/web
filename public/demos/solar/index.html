FAQs

Common questions

Will I still have an electric bill?

Almost always, yes. Most utilities keep a monthly service charge, and many plans charge for power you use at night or bill demand charges. Your report shows which parts of your bill solar can lower and which stay.

What happens during a power outage?

A standard grid-tied solar system shuts off during an outage for line-worker safety, even on a sunny day. To keep power on you need a battery, and for long outages, a generator too. We explain the difference between backing up a few essential circuits and the whole home.

How do I know if my roof is a good fit?

Direction, shade, usable space and the roof's age and condition matter most. The analyzer starts with your roof on a satellite map, and a reviewer checks it before any proposal. If a roof needs replacing soon, we'll tell you to do that first.

Should I pay cash, finance, lease or sign a PPA?

It depends on whether you want to own the system, your tax situation, and how long you'll stay in the home. Cash and loans mean you own it; leases and PPAs mean a company owns it and you pay for it or its power. The financing page compares them side by side.

What if I sell my home?

An owned system usually transfers with the house. A financed system may need to be paid off at sale, and a lease or PPA is transferred to the buyer if they qualify. These terms are in the contract, and we point them out before you sign.

How much maintenance does solar need?

Very little: panels have no moving parts. Monitoring tells you if production drops. Batteries and inverters have their own warranties, and generators need regular service. Your proposal lists who handles each.
