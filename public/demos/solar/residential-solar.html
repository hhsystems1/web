Residential solar, explained

How panels turn sunlight into power for your home, what makes a roof a good fit, and what to expect from equipment, production and installation.

Check my home
How it works

Sunlight in, household power out

  • Panels on your roof turn sunlight into direct current (DC) power.
  • Inverters convert it to the alternating current (AC) your home uses.
  • Your home uses solar first. Extra power goes to the grid or a battery.
  • The grid covers the rest, at night and on cloudy days, and your meter tracks both directions.

Property suitability

Is your roof a good fit?

Direction

South-facing roofs produce the most. East and west work well, typically 10 to 20% less. North-facing sections are usually left empty.

Shade

Trees, chimneys and neighboring buildings cut production. A few hours of afternoon shade can matter more than you'd think.

Roof age and condition

Panels last decades. If your roof needs replacing in the next few years, it's usually cheaper to do it first.

Usable space

Vents, skylights and fire-code setbacks from edges and ridges limit where panels can go. Your layout accounts for them.

Equipment & production

What goes on your home

Panels

Most residential panels today are 400 to 450 watts each. More efficient panels fit more power on a small roof.

Inverters

One central inverter, or a microinverter under each panel so shade on one panel doesn't drag down the rest.

Monitoring

An app that shows production day by day, so a problem gets caught quickly.

Production depends on your location, roof direction and tilt, shade, and the equipment. Panels also lose a small amount of output each year, usually around half a percent. Your report shows the exact figures it uses.

Installation & warranties

What to expect

After design, permits and utility approval, installation is usually one to three days on site. The system turns on after inspection and the utility's permission to operate.

Warranties to compare

Panel product and performance warranties, inverter warranty, and the installer's workmanship and roof-penetration warranty. They vary by manufacturer and installer, so your proposal lists each one with its length.
