Dura-Dressing tire coating

A flexible clear coat for rubber. It dries and cures much like paint, with no oils or silicones, and stays washable, waterproof and non-sticky for months.

A coating, not a dressing

Dura-Dressing works like ceramic coating for your tires: it bonds to clean rubber and stays there.

Dura-Dressing

  • A clear polymer coat that dries like paint
  • No oils or silicone, dry to the touch
  • Washable and waterproof once cured
  • Won't sling onto paint or turn brown
  • Shine lasts months; reload in 1–2 min per tire

Typical tire shine

  • Oil or silicone that sits on top
  • Tacky or greasy finish
  • Rinses off in the first wash or rain
  • Slings onto paint, can brown the tire
  • Reapply every wash

How it works

Prep is the whole game. A clean tire takes the coating; one with old silicone on it won't.

  1. Strip

    Scrub with Dura-Cleaner until the suds stop turning brown. Tires with old dressing usually need 3–4 cleanings.

    About 16 oz cleaner per car

  2. Coat

    Out of direct sun, wipe on a couple of very thin coats with a foam or microfiber pad.

    30 seconds to 2 minutes per tire

  3. Cure

    At 80°F it dries in minutes. Give it 12 hours to fully cure at 75–90°F, 24 hours when it's cooler.

    No sling, no tacky feel once cured

  4. Reload

    Wash, dry, and wipe on a thin top-up coat. No more scrubbing after the first time.

    Every 6–12 weeks on a daily driver

Keep it shining

Once it's on, care takes minutes. Just because the shine dulls doesn't mean the coating is gone.

Do

  • Wash tires and wheels with car wash soap or diluted dish soap, water and a soft brush
  • Deep clean every 2–3 months on a daily driver: Dura-Cleaner on a microfiber pad, 3–4 passes, rinse
  • Reload with a thin coat every 6–10 weeks to renew UV protection and shine
  • Wear gloves, store at 50–75°F, use within 6 months of purchase

Don't

  • Put any other tire dressing over it, including at a dealer service visit
  • Use harsh chemicals on tires or wheels every wash
  • Apply in direct sunlight or below freezing

Questions people ask

From Dura-Coating's own Q&A.

All questions
How much Dura-Dressing will I need?

A standard car uses about 1.5–2 oz, an SUV about 2 oz and a pickup 2–2.5 oz. A 45 ft motor coach or a semi tractor with 6 tires uses about 6 oz.

The single-car kit has 4 oz, the XL kit 8 oz, and the semi/RV kit 16 oz.

Do I really need Dura-Cleaner?

Yes. It was made to strip the silicone and solvents old dressings leave behind. Dura-Dressing has to stick to the tire, not to old dressing.

Plan on about 16 oz for a car, and up to 32 oz or more for an SUV or truck depending on what was on the tires before.

How long does it take to dry?

At 80°F it dries in 1–4 minutes, and 30 minutes of sun per side makes it close to waterproof within an hour. Without sun, allow 12 hours at 75–90°F and 24 hours when cooler.

Don't apply it in direct sunlight or below freezing.

How long does it really last?

The coating stays on for a long time; only the shine needs renewing. A garaged weekend car can shine for about a year. On a daily driver, do a quick reload coat at least every 12 weeks.

It isn't shining, or it came off in a few days. Why?

Every batch is tested for 4 weeks and has to survive 15 cleanings before it ships, so the cause is almost always a tire that still has oil or silicone on it. Clean it again and recoat.

Quick check: wipe a drop on the clean black bottle and let it dry somewhere warm. If it shines there, the product is fine and the tire needs more cleaning. Still stuck? Contact them and they'll help.
